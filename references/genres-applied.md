# Applied genres — grants, monographs, reports, technical documents, popular science, presentations, letters

Read with `SKILL.md` §1d and `references/draft.md` (draft) and §1c (voice), and with `references/genres.md` for journal genres. **The call, the funder's template, the publisher's rules or the customer's contract always win** over everything below. Figures here are marked as a standard, a funder's rule (with its date) or common practice. Funders' rules change: check the current call before relying on any line marked with a date.

Gates A–D apply in every genre. Each section below says what the genre adds to them and which rubric dimensions (`SKILL.md` §2) apply; a dimension marked N/A is left out of the mean, and the report says so.

## 7. Grant application (заявка на грант)

**Job:** convince a reviewer, who reads many applications against fixed criteria, that the work matters, can be done by this team, and will produce something checkable.
**Shape (default, when the call gives none):** the aim and the specific objectives → why it matters now (the problem, the gap, for whom) → what is new → approach, objective by objective, with the main risks and the alternative for each → work plan with milestones → expected results and how they will be measured → team and resources → budget justification → data management and, where asked, ethics. **Mirror the call:** use its headings, its order and its terms, so the reviewer finds each criterion where the form puts it.
**Examples of funders' frameworks (check the current version before use):**
- **NIH (USA):** for most research project grants with due dates from 25 January 2025 the simplified review framework groups the criteria into three factors — Importance of the Research (Significance, Innovation, scored 1–9), Rigor and Feasibility (Approach, scored 1–9), Expertise and Resources (Investigators, Environment, judged sufficient or not). Notice NOT-OD-25-132 (17 July 2025; applications submitted for the 25 September 2025 receipt date and later; checked 17 November 2026): applications substantially developed by AI, or with sections substantially developed by AI, are not considered the applicants' original ideas; each PI may submit at most six applications per calendar year.
- **NSF (USA):** a one-page Project Summary with an Overview, Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts; a Project Description of no more than 15 pages unless the solicitation says otherwise, with a section labelled "Broader Impacts".
- **Horizon Europe (EU):** Part B follows the three award criteria — Excellence, Impact, Quality and efficiency of the implementation; thresholds and weights depend on the call. Applicants may use generative AI tools but must say which tools were used and how; the use itself is not a reason to penalise a proposal (European Commission, standard briefing for expert evaluators, v13.0, 1 December 2025).
- **Other funders, including national foundations:** the call documents (конкурсная документация) define the form, the criteria and any AI rule.
**Funder rules on AI tools (grants):** before drafting, read the funder's rule. Where text drafted with such tools is not accepted as the applicant's own (NIH, above), the skill does not draft narrative sections: it builds the outline, checks the author's own text, and edits only where the author asks and the rule allows. A statement on tools goes into the application only where the funder requires one, in the funder's wording; otherwise the skill adds none.
**Voice:** the first page carries the whole case; every aim is testable; every promise has a measure and a date; no superlatives. "First", "unique", "world-leading" stay out unless a search of the literature and, where relevant, patents supports them — and the search is cited.
**Also check:** eligibility, page and character limits, required annexes, budget arithmetic (Gate B), and — with the institution's grants office — eligibility, export-control and sanctions rules for every partner and payment.
**Typical failures:** a literature review instead of a problem; aims that are activities ("to study…") rather than questions or deliverables; no risks; a work plan that does not match the budget; text copied from the last paper.
**Rubric:** all seven dimensions apply (3 = command of the state of the art). Venue bar: the funder's own scale and thresholds where they exist, mapped to the dimensions and recorded.

## 8. Monograph (монография)

**Job:** give a full and comprehensive account of one problem or topic, by one author or several — the definition of ГОСТ Р 7.0.60-2020 (in force since 1 December 2020).
**Shape:** preface or introduction with the problem, the book's argument and its scope → chapters, each with its own question, its evidence and its conclusions, in an order that builds the argument → a conclusion that answers the problem → references, index, list of abbreviations. Front matter and imprint follow the publisher's rules (in Russia also the ГОСТ for imprint data the publisher names); reviewers' names appear where the publisher requires them.
**Voice:** one line of argument through the book; chapters refer to each other; terms stay the same from chapter to chapter.
**Typical failures:** a dissertation printed as it was; chapters that are earlier papers glued together, each with its own introduction; a literature review that fills half the book; no conclusion beyond a summary of chapters.
**Rubric:** all seven dimensions apply.

## 9. Research report (отчёт о НИР, ГОСТ 7.32-2017)

ГОСТ 7.32-2017 has been in force in Russia since 1 July 2018 (it replaced ГОСТ 7.32-2001). The customer's terms of reference and contract set the content; the standard sets the structure and the form.
**Structural elements (§4):** title page; list of performers; реферат; contents; terms and definitions; list of abbreviations and symbols; introduction; main part; conclusion; list of sources used; appendices. The standard marks which of them are mandatory — check the current text for the report at hand.
**Реферат (§5.3):** the volume of the report (pages, books, figures, tables, sources, appendices); 5 to 15 keywords from the text; the text — object, aim, methods, results and their novelty, field of application, recommendations, efficiency, forecasts (missing parts are left out, the order is kept). Optimum length 850 printed characters, at most one page.
**Introduction (§5.7) and conclusion (§5.9):** the introduction gives the state of the problem, the grounds and the need for the work, its novelty and links to other work; the conclusion gives brief conclusions, how fully the tasks were solved, recommendations and initial data for using the results, the technical and economic efficiency of implementing them, and a comparison with the best results in the field.
**Voice:** official register, impersonal forms customary; still verbs, short sentences and named objects — no канцелярит beyond the formulas the customer requires (`references/voice.md` §2).
**Typical failures:** a реферат that announces instead of reporting results; a conclusion that repeats the introduction; tasks in the conclusion that do not match those in the terms of reference.
**Rubric:** all seven dimensions apply; dimension 1 judges novelty against the terms of reference.

## 10. Technical documentation (техническая документация)

**Job:** let a user do a task, look something up, or understand a system — one of these per document.
**Shape:** the Diátaxis framework (Daniele Procida) separates four types — tutorials (learning by doing), how-to guides (solving a task), reference (facts to look up) and explanation (understanding why); keep them in separate documents or sections and link between them. A how-to guide: the goal → prerequisites → numbered steps, one action each, with the expected result → what to do if it fails.
**Standards:** ISO 24495-1:2023 (plain language) gives governing principles and guidelines for plain-language documents and applies also to technical writing. In Russia text documents of the ЕСКД follow ГОСТ Р 2.105-2019 (general requirements for text documents, approved 29 April 2019, with Amendment No. 1); software and automated-system documents follow the ГОСТ 19 and ГОСТ 34 series where the contract requires them. Quote a clause of these standards only after reading its current text.
**Voice:** imperative steps, present tense; one term per concept, listed in a glossary; requirements with «должен» / "must"; no marketing, no explanation inside a procedure (link to it).
**Typical failures:** a "guide" that mixes a tutorial, reference tables and design history; synonyms for one term; steps that hide two actions; screenshots that no longer match.
**Rubric:** dimensions 2 (statements match the system), 4 (steps reproducible), 5, 7 apply; 6 reads as usefulness for the task; 1 and 3 N/A.

## 11. Popular-science article (научно-популярная статья)

**Job:** let an interested non-specialist understand what was found, how we know it, and what is still unknown — without making it bigger than it is.
**Shape:** the finding and why the reader should care, in the first paragraph → how it was found (who, what was measured, on whom or what) → what it means and what it does not → open questions → the source (journal, year, DOI or link). An opening scene, quotation or example is used only if the author supplies a real one.
**Exaggeration check (extends Gate D):** causal claims from correlational data, inference to humans from animal or cell studies, and advice that goes beyond the paper are flagged. In a study of 462 press releases from 20 UK universities and the related news, exaggeration in the press release was strongly associated with exaggeration in the news, and there was little evidence that it increased news uptake (Sumner et al., *BMJ* 2014; 349: g7015; doi:10.1136/bmj.g7015).
**Voice:** explain each term once or replace it; numbers in terms the reader can picture, with the comparison stated; one analogy at most per idea, checked with the author for accuracy; quotations only real and approved by the person quoted.
**Typical failures:** "учёные доказали" / "scientists have proven"; "breakthrough", "cure", "сенсация"; no source; no limitation; a mouse study presented as a human result.
**Rubric:** dimensions 2 (claims match the study), 5, 6 (reach) and 7 apply; 1, 3, 4 N/A (they judge the underlying research, not the article).

## 12. Scientific talk and slides (научный доклад, презентация)

**Job:** help an audience follow one argument while the speaker talks.
**Shape:** a title slide that states the result → the question → one slide per step of the argument → limitations and what the work does not claim → the take-home message and contacts. Each slide: a sentence headline that states its point, supported by visual evidence — a figure, a photo, a diagram — with text only where needed. This is the assertion–evidence design; with 110 engineering students it gave better comprehension, fewer misconceptions and lower perceived cognitive load than common slide defaults (Garner & Alley, *International Journal of Engineering Education* 2013, 29(6)).
**Rules:** every number on a slide traces to a source or to the authors' data (Gates A–B); figures have the right to be shown (licence, permission); the speaker notes carry what is said, not the slide.
**Typical failures:** topic headlines ("Results"); slides of bullet points read aloud; one slide carrying three messages; tables too small to read.
**Rubric:** dimensions 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 apply; 3 and 4 N/A for the slides (judge them in the paper).

## 13. Business or partner presentation (бизнес-научная презентация)

**Job:** help a decision-maker — a partner, an investor, a manager, an agency — decide one thing.
**Shape (common practice for decision-makers):** the answer first — what you ask for and why, on the first one or two slides → the problem in the listener's terms → the evidence and how strong it is → the solution and its maturity → the value, with numbers and their assumptions → risks and how they are handled → the request: what, how much, by when.
**Maturity:** say how mature the technology or the research is, in a named scale where the listener uses one — for example the technology readiness levels of the EU programmes, from TRL 1 (basic principles observed) to TRL 9 (actual system proven in operational environment).
**Voice:** short sentence headlines; numbers with sources and dates; forecasts as ranges with their assumptions; no superlatives; "first" only after a search; nothing confidential or under someone else's rights.
**Typical failures:** twenty slides before the request; market sizes with no source; a technology described as finished when it is a prototype.
**Rubric:** dimensions 1 (novelty claim checked), 2, 5, 6, 7 apply; 3 and 4 N/A.

## 14. Letters around a publication (сопроводительное письмо, ответ рецензентам, рецензия)

- **Cover letter to the editor (common practice):** what the paper shows in two or three sentences, why it suits this journal, statements the journal requires (originality, no parallel submission, conflicts of interest and any others it lists); suggested or excluded reviewers if the journal asks. One page.
- **Response to reviewers (common practice):** each comment quoted or numbered → the answer → what changed and where (page, line, section). Thank once, at the top. Disagree with evidence, not tone. Every change is in the manuscript, not only in the letter.
- **Review of someone else's manuscript (рецензия, отзыв):** what the paper claims, in two or three sentences → major issues (each with what would resolve it) → minor issues → recommendation. Never upload a confidential manuscript to a tool the journal or funder does not allow.
**Rubric:** dimensions 2, 5, 7 apply; the others N/A.
