# Voice — natural prose in the register of the genre

Read with `SKILL.md` §1c before every voice pass, in every genre. The target is prose a careful specialist would write for this reader: precise, concrete, in the register of the genre, with nothing that shows it was assembled from a template. The pass changes wording only; facts, numbers, citations, hedges and limitations stay as they are (§1c, "Facts are frozen"). All examples below are synthetic.

## 1. The register follows the genre

| Genre | Reader | Target voice | Keep | Cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Article, review, theses, extended abstract | specialist | classic academic: the author shows what was found, as far as the evidence goes | authorial «мы» / "we" for the authors' actions; hedges sized to the evidence | announcements, summary tails, stock phrases |
| Monograph | specialist and neighbouring fields | the same, with one line of argument through the chapters | the recurring question; cross-references between chapters | chapters that read as separate papers; the same introduction in every chapter |
| Grant application | reviewer reading many applications | confident and specific; every claim checkable | the funder's headings, terms and criteria | hype; promises without a measure or a date |
| Research report (ГОСТ 7.32) | customer, expert | official register; impersonal forms are customary | the contract's terms; «должен» in requirements | канцелярит beyond what the standard or the contract asks |
| Technical documentation | a user doing a task | plain and direct: imperative steps, present tense, second person where the house style allows | one term for one concept | synonyms for a term; marketing; explanation inside a procedure |
| Popular science | interested non-specialist | conversational and concrete; says how we know and what is not yet known | the uncertainty; the source | unexplained jargon; hype; scenes or quotes the author did not supply |
| Talk slides | audience listening to a speaker | a sentence headline per slide that states the point; the speaker says the rest | one message per slide | bullet lists of noun phrases |
| Business or partner presentation | decision-maker | the answer and the request first, then the evidence | numbers with their assumptions and sources | superlatives; "world-first" claims nobody has checked |

Official does not mean канцелярит. A report or a specification can be formal and still use verbs, short sentences and named subjects.

Japanese texts: register, wordiness and directness follow `references/language.md` §3; the markers below are Russian and English.

## 2. Канцелярит (Russian)

Корней Чуковский coined the term in «Живой как жизнь» (1962); Нора Галь built an editor's method against it in «Слово живое и мёртвое» (1972). Typical forms and plain replacements:

| Pattern | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Split predicate: «проводить», «осуществлять», «производить», «выполнять», «оказывать» + an action noun | «Был проведён анализ проб» | «Мы проанализировали пробы» |
| A chain of genitives | «повышение эффективности использования водных ресурсов» | «воду расходуют экономнее» — with the subject named |
| «является», «представляет собой» as a mere link | «Озеро является мелководным» | «Озеро мелкое» |
| «данный», «вышеуказанный», «вышеизложенный» | «данный метод» | «этот метод», or the method's name |
| «в целях», «в рамках», «в части», «по линии», «имеет место» | «в целях снижения нагрузки» | «чтобы снизить нагрузку» |
| A passive that hides who did it, when it matters | «было установлено, что…» | «мы установили…» / «Иванов [3] показал…» |
| Empty intensifiers | «весьма значительный рост» | the number: «рост на 40 %» |

Keep what the genre requires: the terms of a standard, the wording of a contract, the formulas of requirements («должен», «не допускается»).

## 3. Template patterns — шаблонные обороты (Russian and English)

These are editorial markers of template-driven writing — text produced in a hurry or from a template. None of them says who wrote a text, and the editor never draws that conclusion. For English, Kobak et al. (2025) measured excess style words across more than 15 million PubMed abstracts; for Russian no comparable corpus study was found at the last review, so the Russian list is an editor's checklist, not a measured one. One such word in one text proves nothing; each marker is a prompt to rewrite. The rule for every replacement: name the concrete thing the phrase hides — the number, the method, the place, the source — or delete the phrase.

**Words and stock frames.**
RU: «в современном мире», «в эпоху цифровизации», «не секрет, что», «играет ключевую роль», «открывает новые возможности», «комплексный подход», «широкий спектр», «многогранный», «уникальный», «инновационный», «нельзя не отметить», «важно подчеркнуть», «в заключение можно сказать», «подводя итог», «давайте рассмотрим».
EN: the style words with excess frequency in 2024 PubMed abstracts ("delve", "underscore", "showcase", "crucial", "pivotal", "intricate", "notably", "comprehensive", "additionally", "insights"; Kobak et al. 2025) and the frames "in today's fast-paced world", "plays a crucial role", "a testament to", "in the realm of", "navigate the complexities", "unlock the potential", "it is worth noting", "let's dive into".

**Constructions.**

| Pattern | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Contrast frame, repeated (one is fine where the contrast is itself the claim) | «Это не просто модель, а инструмент управления» / "It's not just a model — it's a tool" | «Модель показывает, какие решения уменьшат ущерб» — state the positive claim |
| Triads by reflex | «точный, надёжный и масштабируемый метод» | the one property the data support, with its number |
| A rhetorical question answered at once | «Почему это важно? Потому что…» | the answer as a statement |
| Announcement | «В этом разделе мы рассмотрим…» / "This section explores…" | start with the content |
| Summary tail | a paragraph ending «Таким образом, …» that restates itself | end on the most specific point |
| Vague attribution | «Исследования показывают…», "Experts agree…" — no citation | cite the source or cut the claim (a Gate A item) |
| Stacked hedges | «может потенциально способствовать» / "may potentially help" | one hedge, sized to the evidence |
| Filler conclusion | «Требуются дальнейшие исследования» and nothing else | which study, of what, to answer which question |
| False balance | «с одной стороны… с другой стороны…» with no conclusion | say which side the evidence favours, or that it does not decide |

**Layout.** Headings, bold type and bullet lists used in place of an argument; colon labels («Ключевой вывод:», "Key takeaway:"); emoji; every paragraph the same length; every section opening with the same move. Prose genres carry their logic in sentences; lists are for items that really are parallel (materials, steps, criteria).

## 4. No template echo (draft mode)

1. Outlines (`assets/outlines.md`) and moves (`references/sections.md`) are scaffolding. Their labels («Актуальность», «Цель», "Significance", "Territory → gap → aim") appear in the text only where the venue asks for those headings.
2. A section opens with its content, not with its label in sentence form («Актуальность темы обусловлена…»).
3. Sections do not all open the same way, and paragraphs are as long as their content needs.
4. The author's own sentences from the core (outlines §0) stay close to verbatim: they are the voice of the text. The connecting text adapts to them, not the other way round.

## 5. What "natural" does not mean

- No invented anecdotes, scenes, personal experience, quotations, examples, jokes or opinions that the author did not supply, not even to liven the text up. If the genre needs one (a popular-science opening, say), ask the author for a real one.
- No deliberate errors, slang or colloquialisms: they lower the quality and misrepresent the text. The editor makes no promises about how any tool will classify a text.
- No loss of precision: terms, units, hedges and limitations stay.
- The author's own phrasing stays when it is clear, even if it is unusual. The voice belongs to the author, not to a house style.

## 6. Check after the pass

1. `python3 mse_style.py text.docx` — the markers of §2–3 that a script can count: authorial voice, rhythm, glue punctuation, nominalizations, stock phrases, contrast frames, summary tails, vague attributions without a citation, split predicates, uniform paragraph length. With `--reference`, compare with the author's own earlier published texts. In technical documentation and ГОСТ reports ignore the voice flag (impersonal forms are customary there).
2. `python3 mse_diff.py before.docx after.docx` — no NEW NUMBER, NEW CITATION or MISSING NUMBER.
3. Read one paragraph from each section aloud: does it sound like this author talking to this genre's reader?
4. Report three before → after pairs from the author's own text, each with the rule it shows.
